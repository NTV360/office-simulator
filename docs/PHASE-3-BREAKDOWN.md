# Phase 3 breakdown: accounts and takeover

**Status: in progress. Steps 0 to 5 are done; steps 6 to 8 are next.** This turns phase 3 of [MULTIPLAYER-PLAN.md](MULTIPLAYER-PLAN.md#17-phased-plan) into small, ordered, individually testable steps, the same way phases 1 and 2 were done.

**Done when (from the plan, as changed on 2026-10-09: accounts are made by admins):** an admin makes your account, you choose your password, create your character, log in and drive your person, log out and watch it carry on as an NPC, and log back in where it is.

## 1. What changes, in plain words

Until now every browser is an anonymous viewer of one office. After phase 3:

- **Nobody connects without an account, and accounts are made by admins.** Log in, get a one-time ticket, open the realtime connection with it.
- **A new account waits for a desk.** It can log in and walk around as a **guest** (appears at the entrance, nothing is saved about the guest's position), but it has no desk and no person of its own. An admin assigns it one of the unclaimed desks.
- **A claimed desk's person belongs to the account.** When the owner is offline the person is an ordinary autopilot NPC (name and look are the account's). When the owner logs in, the account takes over **that same person, where it stands**. On logout (after a 30 second grace for dropped connections) the person goes back to AI from where it stands.
- **Players are never sent home** by the end-of-day reset; autopilot people still are.
- **Admins are accounts with a role**, not a shared token. They assign and release desks, reset passwords, disable accounts, kick, and still control staff count, speed, pause and announcements.

The movement part is deliberately plain: the browser sends **inputs** (a move direction, heading, run), the server moves the person with the shared collision code and speed limits, and the browser draws what comes back. It works but feels a little laggy (about one round trip). **Prediction and reconciliation arrive in phase 4**, which is where "many people walk together smoothly" is solved.

## 2. Decisions

Approved by you (the defaults proposed earlier):

1. Registration creates an account **waiting for a desk**; an **admin picks the slot**.
2. Waiting accounts **can log in as a guest** and walk around from the entrance.
3. **No email.** An admin resets a forgotten password.
4. **Admin is a role** on an account, replacing the shared `ADMIN_TOKEN`.
5. **Plain SQL migrations stay** (no Prisma yet).

Made by me while planning (say if you disagree):

6. **Cookie sessions instead of access plus refresh tokens.** Login sets one httpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie holding an opaque random session id (stored hashed in the database, 7 days, revocable). It is simpler than the plan's token pair and equally safe for an internal app; the cookie gets the Secure flag as soon as the site is on HTTPS (phase 7). The realtime connection uses a one-time **ticket** (about 30 seconds) from `POST /api/play/ticket`, never a token in a URL.
7. **There is no sign-up (changed 2026-10-09, after step 4).** Everyone in the office is assigned a station, so admins make the accounts (`POST /api/admin/users`) with a first password, and the person must replace it at first login (the server refuses a play ticket until they do). `POST /api/auth/register` and `SIGNUP_CODE` are gone.
8. **The first admin comes from the environment.** `ADMIN_USERNAME` and `ADMIN_PASSWORD` create an admin at start-up if there is none.
9. **argon2id through `@node-rs/argon2`** (prebuilt for Alpine, no compiler in the image). Step 1 proves it works in the Docker image before anything depends on it.
10. **Taking over an away person:** if the owner logs in before their person has come in for the day, the person appears at the entrance and is in (it counts as their arrival). A **guest** is a person with no desk, created at login and removed after logout.
11. **Assigning a desk to someone who is logged in as a guest** disconnects them with the message "your desk was assigned, log in again" (simple and robust, instead of converting a live guest into a staff person).
12. **After a server restart everyone is offline**: claimed persons come back on autopilot and players log in again (the session cookie still works, so it is one click).
13. **Anonymous viewing is removed in online mode** (company only). Offline mode (a private copy, for development) is unchanged.

Still open from the plan, with the plan's defaults (none of them blocks this phase):

- Rename "Desk 01 to 08" as Stations A to H. The plan said "in phase 1" but it was not done; it is cosmetic, so it waits until you want it.
- Moderation (mute, ban): admins. Chat itself is phase 4.
- Remote play over a VPN: office network only for now.

## 5. How each step is checked

Every step runs the earlier checks (`npm test`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`, `npm run check:docs`, `npm run verify:browser:thorough`, `npm run check:mutations`, `npm run test:db`, the Docker smoke test, `VERIFY_URL=http://localhost:8080 VERIFY_ADMIN_TOKEN=local-admin-token npm run verify:browser`, `npm run e2e`) plus its own proof above. The offline recordings must not change in any step: phase 3 adds accounts around the simulation, it does not alter the simulation. Each step gets a "what it turned out to need" note here, and a read-only review (`claude-alt`) of the security-sensitive ones (1, 2, 3, 4, 7).

## 6. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Passwords and sessions are the first security-critical code in the project | argon2id; opaque hashed sessions; constant-time checks; lockouts; nothing sensitive in logs or responses; tests for each failure; a dedicated review before the phase is called done |
| `isStaff` is used in about 20 places with three different meanings | Step 0 does only this, with the recordings as the safety net, before any account exists |
| A player's person is also saved world state: a bug could lose an owner link or someone's look | The save is versioned and validated; a test restores every combination (claimed offline, claimed after a restart, released, account deleted) |
| Plain input-then-wait movement feels laggy | Accepted for this phase and said so; phase 4 is prediction |
| `@node-rs/argon2` fails in the Alpine image | Proved first (step 1) before anything depends on it; the fallback is the `argon2` package with the build tools in the build stage only |
| A dropped connection makes the character wander off | The 30 s grace with an immediate resume |
| Two tabs of one account | A second login kicks the first (tested at the socket) |
| Anyone on the network making accounts | There is no sign-up: only an admin (ADMIN_TOKEN, later an admin login) makes accounts, and a new account has no power until an admin gives it a desk |

## 7. Not in phase 3

Chat and emotes, interactions other than sitting (using the piano, throwing), moving furniture, prediction and reconciliation, voice, and HTTPS. The protocol and the person model are shaped so none of these needs rework.

## 8. What each step turned out to need

### Step 0

1. **Ids are "the lowest id nobody is using"** (`allocatePersonId` in `sim/state.ts`), for staff and guests alike, instead of the planned monotonic counter plus a high range for guests. A counter would climb forever as an admin adds and removes slots and eventually hit the protocol's 16-bit limit; a fixed guest range would have to be sized. Lowest-free keeps ids as small as the biggest crowd has been, can never give two living people the same id (a test churns 400 random joins, leaves and slot changes and checks every time), and a person keeps their id for as long as they exist. The client already drops a person on `leave`, so reuse is safe.
2. **Three predicates replace `isStaff`:** `isAi` (steps them, picks them for meetings, sends them home at night), `hasSlot` (counts in the ledger, the slot number, the fingerprint and the save), `isDriven` (a human drives them). Every one of the 40-odd uses was looked at: the day reset and meetings use `isAi`; the ledger, the slider, `/api/world`, the admin settings and the save use `hasSlot`; the broadcaster sends driven people every tick. On the page, clicking, random follow and the pose of "you" use `isLocalPlayer` (is it the person *this* page controls?) instead, because once other humans are drawn, "driven" no longer means "me".
3. **`removeStaff` and `setStaffCount` never remove a person who belongs to an account** (or one a human is driving); the count may stop above the target.
4. **Save version 2** carries each person's `owner` (account id or null). Version 1 saves still load, as a world with no owners; this was exercised for real, because the Docker stack's database held a version 1 save from before. A person who was online when the server stopped is saved as autopilot (nobody is online after a restart); guests are not saved. Unknown versions and owners that are not whole account ids are refused.
5. **Tests:** 15 new (`sim/identity.test.ts`) covering ids, the three questions, slot removal rules, the day reset, meetings, and the save in every combination, plus one in the broadcaster. The offline recordings, the mutation check, the database tests, the Docker smoke test, the browser runner against the Docker site and `npm run e2e` all pass unchanged.

### Step 1

1. **What exists.** `POST /api/auth/register` (since removed: see "Change after step 4"), `login`, `logout`, `password` and `GET /api/auth/me` (`apps/server/src/auth/`); migration `002_accounts.sql` with `accounts`, `sessions` and `audit_log`; the first admin from `ADMIN_USERNAME` and `ADMIN_PASSWORD`; an optional `SIGNUP_CODE`. The auth rules (`AuthService`) are separate from storage (`AccountStore`: PostgreSQL in production, an in-memory twin for fast tests) and from HTTP (`AuthController`), so each is tested at its own level: 59 unit and HTTP tests, plus 7 against a real database.
2. **Passwords.** argon2id through `@node-rs/argon2` at 19 MiB, 2 passes, 1 lane (the OWASP minimum). It loads and hashes inside the Alpine Docker image (checked: the first admin is created there and its stored hash starts `$argon2id$v=19$m=19456`). A policy: 8 to 128 characters, not the username, not a short list of common ones, not one repeated character. An unknown username still costs one hash verification, and that dummy hash is made at start-up, so wrong names and wrong passwords take the same time.
3. **Sessions.** A random 256-bit secret in an `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict` cookie (`Secure` when `COOKIE_SECURE=true` or a trusted proxy says the page is https); only its SHA-256 is stored. A session ends after 7 days unused, or 30 days after it was made however much it is used. Changing your password ends your other sessions; disabling an account ends all of them.
4. **Login limits.** 5 wrong passwords for a name from one address in 15 minutes lock **that address** out of that name; 25 from anywhere lock the name for everyone as a last resort. Per-address limits: 30 logins a minute, 5 sign-ups per 10 minutes, 5 wrong current passwords per 15 minutes when changing a password. Addresses are counted by IPv6 /64 so rotating addresses inside one network does not help. The same limits apply to names that do not exist, so a lock cannot be used to find out which names are real.
5. **Other sites cannot forge a request.** State-changing auth routes refuse a request whose Origin is another site, and accept only JSON bodies (a plain HTML form gets 415). A damaged cookie is just "not logged in".
6. **A second pair of eyes found things worth fixing, all fixed with a test:** a stranger could lock the real owner out of an account (now the lock is per address); a login username had no length limit and a NUL byte reached the database (now anything that cannot be a username is refused before it touches a counter or the database); a cookie with bad percent-encoding gave a 500; IPv6 users could dodge the limits; password change had no attempt limit; sessions never had an absolute lifetime; the dummy hash was slow the first time. Two points are accepted as they are: registration says "that username is taken" (it has to, for a company app on its own network), and a session cookie's server-side lifetime is 7 days idle.
7. **Nothing secret is logged or returned.** Tests check that no response contains the password, a hash or the session token (except the cookie that carries it), and that none of them appears in the server log, even for failed requests.
8. **Without a database** the account routes answer 503 instead of pretending. Offline mode (a private copy for development) has no accounts.

### Step 2

1. **The flow.** A logged-in page asks `POST /api/play/ticket` (session cookie, JSON only, not from another site, 10 a minute per account) and gets a 24-byte random ticket that works **once** for **30 seconds** (`auth/tickets.ts`). The first thing it says on the socket is `hello { version, ticket }` (protocol version 3). The server uses the ticket up, checks the session is still alive and the account is enabled, and only then admits the connection, takes the account's one slot (a second login kicks the first with "you logged in from somewhere else"), and sends the welcome. Anonymous viewing is gone; with no database the gateway says "accounts are not available" instead.
2. **A connection lives and dies with its session.** Logging out, changing the password (the connections of the other sessions go; the one you changed it from stays), disabling an account, or an expired session ends the connection. Ending is immediate for logout and password change (the auth service announces it), and a **periodic check once a minute** catches expiry and disabling for sockets that are already open (`SESSION_SWEEP_MS`).
3. **A review found races, now closed and tested:** a logout landing while a connection was still being admitted could leave a joined socket with a dead session (the socket now belongs to its account and session from the moment its ticket is redeemed, so the logout finds it); a flood of silent connections could fill the server (at most 20 waiting-to-say-hello connections per address, `MAX_UNJOINED_PER_ADDRESS`; people who have joined do not count, so a whole office behind one address is fine); the client sent a person to the login screen for any ticket failure, even a busy server (now only a real 401 does; anything else waits and retries); two overlapping login attempts could open two screens (now one at a time); a disabled account's kick no longer says so (it says the session ended).
4. **The page has a login and sign-up screen (the sign-up part was removed after step 4)** (`apps/client/src/net/login.js`, plain DOM, text only so nothing from the server can inject markup), an account box with the user name and **Log out**, and reconnects by itself with a fresh ticket each time. A server restart does not log anyone out (sessions are in the database). This is deliberately plain; step 6 builds the full flow (waiting for a desk, character creation, driving).
5. **Checks changed with it.** The smoke test logs in as a smoke account, asks for a ticket, connects through the proxy, and also checks that a connection without a ticket, and a ticket used twice, are turned away. The browser runner logs its pages in through the API (a different account for each page, since an account has one connection) and has a new section that uses the login screen like a person: a weak password is refused on screen, registering lands in the office, logging out clears it, a wrong password gets the plain message, and a second browser logging in as the same account takes over and the first is told why. `npm run e2e` logs its three browsers in the same way.
6. **Server tests grew a helper** (`apps/server/src/test-support.ts`): boot the real server with accounts in memory, register or log in, mint a ticket, open a socket and say hello.

### Step 3

1. **Two halves.** The simulation half is in `packages/shared/src/sim/takeover.ts`: `takeControl(person)`, `handBack(person)`, `makeGuest`, `removeGuest`, `setLook`. The decision half is the server's `PlayerManager` (`apps/server/src/play/`): *when* to use them (a login, a dropped connection, an admin's choice). Each half is tested alone (21 simulation tests; 19 manager tests with controllable timers) and together through real logins and sockets (11 tests).
2. **Taking over.** The person stays exactly where they are: whatever they were doing ends through its normal end hook (props put down, a shared seat freed), they leave any meeting (even as the speaker) and anyone who was chatting with them forgets it. Someone who has not come in yet, or has gone home, walks in at the entrance and that counts as their arrival. Nothing in the simulation steps them, sends them home at night, or puts them in a meeting while a human drives them.
3. **Handing back.** After 30 seconds without a connection (`GRACE_MS`) the person is idle where they stand and the simulation picks what they do next; late in the day they head home. A reconnect inside the grace period gets the very same person and nothing visible happens in between. A second tab taking over does not hand the person back in between either (the old connection's drop is ignored because it is no longer the account's connection).
4. **Guests.** An account with no desk gets a new person at the entrance for as long as it is connected (plus the grace period), seen by everyone, with an id that is free while they are there. A guest is never saved. "Handing back" a guest removes them (there is no desk to go back to), which a randomised test found when it crashed the simulation.
5. **Admin gives and takes desks** (still through the shared `ADMIN_TOKEN` until step 7): `GET /api/admin/users`, `GET /api/admin/slots`, `POST /api/admin/users/:id/assign-slot { "spot": "desk:12" }`, `POST /api/admin/users/:id/release-slot`. A desk can be given only if a person sits at it, belongs to nobody, and is not Hazel (her look and leaving time are special). The person becomes the account's (renamed to the account, with the account's look once it has one). An account connected as a guest when a desk is assigned is told "your desk was assigned, log in again"; one playing a desk that is taken away is handed back and told. The database decides who has which desk (one account per desk, enforced by a unique key).
6. **The real office seats its 40 people at random desks out of 70**, so an admin gives out the desks that are occupied (the slots list shows which); to give more accounts a desk, raise the number of slots first.
7. **The accounts win at start-up.** After the world is restored, every account's desk is made to belong to its person, and a person who still claims to belong to an account that no longer has that desk becomes an ordinary NPC again. Owners are also in the saved world, so a restart brings the same desks back (checked against a real database).
8. **Viewers.** The welcome now says which person is yours (`you`). A takeover, a hand-back, a rename or a new look is sent as a `person` update, and the viewer's copy updates the same object in place, so a taken-over person keeps their body and pose; only a changed look rebuilds the body.
9. **Mutation check** grew four entries (a takeover that does not stop the task, a hand-back that leaves the human in charge, a takeover that always teleports, a guest that is not removed); all are caught.

### Review of step 3, and what it changed

A read-only review found no corruption of the simulation's references (partners, meetings, seats), but several problems in the layers around it, all fixed with a test:

1. **Hazel could be claimed** through the database (a desk assignment that bypassed the admin check): reconcile and login now refuse her desk (a login plays as a guest instead).
2. **A desk could be assigned to a person who was removed in the meantime** (the slot count lowered while the database write was in flight): the person is re-checked after the write, the assignment is rolled back, and viewers are not told about a ghost.
3. **Two admin calls at once** could give one account two desks, or two accounts one desk: logins and desk changes now run one at a time, and the database's unique key is the final word.
4. **A login during a release** could leave someone driving a person who was no longer theirs: the release writes the database first and then changes the world with nothing awaited in between, and a login re-reads the account instead of trusting what the gateway read earlier.
5. **A join that failed half way** (after the person was taken over) left the account "online" for ever: it now starts the grace period like any dropped connection.
6. **A huge account id in the address** gave a database error (500): it is a 400 now. A release that finds nobody at the desk but clears the database now succeeds.
7. **A person played across a day change kept yesterday's schedule**: handing them back on a later day gives them today's.
8. **A released person kept the account's name**: they get an NPC name again (no random numbers used).

Left as they are: the staff count can sit above the number an admin set when claimed people exist (they are never removed, by design), and the slots list shows exactly who is where.

### Step 4

1. **Two new messages (protocol version 4).** `input { seq, mx, mz, heading, run }`: which way to go in the world (a fraction of full speed), which way to face, run or walk; about 20 a second. `act { sit | stand }`. The player never sends a position, so it cannot be anywhere the rules do not allow. Both are refused before `hello`; a joined connection may send 60 messages a second (a stream of inputs plus the occasional ping and act), an unjoined one 20.
2. **How movement works** (`packages/shared/src/sim/driven.ts`, run by the server at the start of every tick before the simulation steps): the latest input wins; its direction is shortened to at most length 1 whatever was sent; walking is 1.5 m/s and running 3 m/s of **real** time (the clock speed does not speed a player up); the move goes through the same collision code the browser used (`stepPlayer`), in steps of at most a quarter of a metre so a slow tick cannot jump over a thin wall; an input is believed for a quarter of a second (at any tick rate) and then they stand still, so a dropped or stalled client does not keep walking; a resting thumb (under 8 percent) does nothing. A message numbered lower than the last is ignored, and a new connection starts its numbering again.
3. **Sitting.** `sit` takes the nearest seat within 1.15 m that is free (a shared seat nobody is in; a desk that is theirs, or whose owner is away, and that no one else is sitting at) and has a clear way to it (walls and furniture block; the last 0.6 m, the seat's own furniture, is ignored). It puts them in it facing its way, with the right pose and what they hold (a phone in a booth, a mug on a bar stool, the game pad, the guitar). `stand` puts them at the seat's approach point. Moving gets them out of the chair first. A seat a human is in is not used by the simulation, and the owner of a desk someone else sits at waits. Disconnecting, a second tab, an admin taking the desk, or a guest leaving all free the seat.
4. **A review found real holes, all closed with tests:** sit and stand could be chained to hop about faster than running (now one accepted sit or stand a second, and sitting needs a clear way to the seat, so a seat behind a wall is out of reach); two humans could share a desk with no occupant tracking (every seat has one now, and the simulation respects it); a reconnect kept the old message numbering and locked the new connection out (now it starts fresh); after a world reset old inputs looked fresh (an input from the future is not believed); movement while the clock is paused is **kept on purpose** (a paused office is frozen but players can still walk about); low tick rates could tunnel through walls (the tick rate now has a floor of 10 and movement is in short steps); the stale-input window was counted in ticks (now a quarter of a second at any rate).
5. **Verified:** 33 movement and seat tests in the simulation, 18 through real sockets (1.5 and 3 m/s measured over a second, a million-times input still capped, stopping after the player stops sending, being seen moving by others, sitting, two players one seat, standing up by walking, a drop while seated freeing the seat after the grace period, flood handling), 5 more manager tests, and 6 new mutation entries (all 19 caught).
6. **Feel.** The page does not drive yet (step 6), so nothing here is visible in a browser. When it does, expect about one round trip of delay between pressing a key and moving; phase 4 adds prediction so your own movement feels instant.

### Change after step 4: no self-registration (2026-10-09)

1. **Why.** Everybody in the office is assigned one station, so accounts are made by admins, not by the people. The user then changes the first password later.
2. **What changed.** `POST /api/auth/register`, the sign-up code (`SIGNUP_CODE`) and the per-address sign-up limit are gone. `AuthService.createAccount` (no session, no limit) is used by the new admin route `POST /api/admin/users { username, password }`, which marks the account "must change password". `POST /api/play/ticket` answers 403 `must-change-password` until the person has used `POST /api/auth/password`. The login screen has no sign-up tab; after a first login it shows a "choose your password" step (two fields, same rules as the server) before the office appears.
3. **Checks changed with it.** Test servers make accounts through the service (`useAuth` / `sessionFor` in `test-support.ts`); the smoke test, `verify:browser` and `e2e` make theirs through the admin route (`SMOKE_ADMIN_TOKEN` / `VERIFY_ADMIN_TOKEN`) and then choose a password, like a real person. New tests: the register address is gone, admin-made accounts must change their password before they can get a ticket, duplicates and weak passwords are refused with the reason, and the browser walks through the first-login password step.
4. **Still to do (step 7).** The admin page for making accounts, and resetting a forgotten password (which will set "must change" again). Until then an admin uses the API with the `ADMIN_TOKEN`.

### Step 5

1. **The API** (`apps/server/src/auth/character.controller.ts`): `GET /api/character` gives your saved look (or `null`) and the starting look; `PUT /api/character { "spec": { ... } }` saves one. The account always comes from the session, never from the request, so nobody can change anyone else's look (a test sends another account's id and it is ignored). It needs a login, JSON, the same origin, and an account that has already chosen its own password; it is limited to 10 changes a minute (each one is announced to every viewer).
2. **Always valid.** Whatever is sent goes through `normalizePlayerSpec` (`packages/shared/src/character/spec.ts`): `normalizeSpec` (a wrong colour, style or type falls back to the default, the height is held to 0.9 to 1.1, unknown fields are dropped) and then the looks that belong to Hazel (skirt, cube head, angry face) are removed. A body that is not an object with a `spec` is a 400 and nothing is saved.
3. **No new `look` message.** The plan had one, but step 3 already sends a `person` update whenever a person's look changes (`setLook`), and the viewer rebuilds just that body. So `PlayerManager.applyLook(accountId, spec)` finds the person that is the account's (playing now, or on autopilot at their desk) and calls `setLook`; the account row keeps the look for next time (`attach` and `claim` already read it).
4. **The page** (`apps/client/src/net/creator.js`): swatches from the shared palettes (skin, hair, shirt, trousers, shoes, optional jacket and headphones), seven hair styles, glasses, long sleeves, a height slider, and a live 3D preview made with the real character rig in its own small scene (drag to turn it). It opens by itself, and cannot be skipped, on the first login of an account that has a desk and no look yet; afterwards the **Character** button next to the account name opens it (Cancel and Escape close it without saving).
5. **Verified.** 14 server tests (bad input becomes valid, Hazel's looks dropped, junk bodies, prototype tricks, login/CSRF/JSON/rate limit/must-change-password, own account only, a viewer sees a change without reconnecting, a saved look is the one you arrive with, a desk person on autopilot changes look too). Two deliberate breaks were caught (no `applyLook`; Hazel's looks allowed). In the browser: the page opens on first login, cannot be skipped, the preview draws and changes at once, saving shows the look on your own person and in a second browser, Cancel changes nothing, a reload does not ask again.
6. **Guests** (an account with no desk) are not made to create a character; they can use the **Character** button whenever they like, and the look is kept for the day they get a desk.

**Review of the no-registration change and step 5** (`claude-alt`, read-only): no way found to get an account or play without an admin, or around the must-change-password rule. Done from it: the websocket join also refuses an account that must change its password (a ticket minted before an admin reset), and `GET /api/character` follows the same rule as `PUT`. Left as it is, on purpose: a first password an admin hands out never expires (admins should pass it on in person; the login lockouts limit guessing), and the character-change limiter is per account and in memory (10 a minute, which is the real cap on look broadcasts). When the admin reset-password route arrives in step 7 it must end the account's sessions and open connections (`endAllSessions`).
